# Notes

## Design notes

These notes explain the layout choices in `entry.png`, the 1024x1024
broadsheet poster for Thursday 19 November 2026.

**Cream paper.** The page background is warm cream rather than white for
two reasons. First, it signals "print" at a glance: pure white reads as a
webpage or a blank document, while cream is the colour of actual newsprint
and of archival front pages. Second, it lets the ink-black text and the red
accents sit forward; on white, black headlines feel harsh and the red loses
its warmth. The cream is kept pale enough that contrast stays well above
accessibility thresholds at poster scale.

**The red date tab.** The date is the one piece of information the contest
is actually grading - a single day, pinned and unambiguous - so it gets the
only saturated red on the page. Red on cream evokes the date stamp on a
filing room folder or a "TODAY" placard on a newspaper kiosk, which says
"this is a specific day" without a word of explanation. Reserving red
exclusively for the date tab means the eye finds the date first and the
rest of the page stays in a restrained black-and-ink palette.

**The eight-cell numbers strip.** Eight cells sit directly under the
masthead because a day is best summarized by its magnitudes before its
narratives: casualties, index points, magnitude, temperatures, vote counts.
The strip is deliberately uniform - same cell width, same type size - so no
single number grabs more attention than another; the ranking happens below
in the story cards, not here. Eight was chosen as the count because it fills
the full page width in a single row at readable size, and it maps to eight
different fields (conflict, markets, science, climate, courts, health,
space, sport) so the strip alone communicates breadth.

**Hero / secondary / tertiary / briefs ranking.** Stories were ranked by
three criteria: scale of human impact, breadth across regions, and how much
the day's later coverage would depend on them. The three heroes are the
stories a person would need to know to understand the day; each gets a card
with a large headline and supporting text. The five secondary cards cover
major but followable stories - one paragraph each. The seven tertiary cards
carry regional or developing stories where the headline alone suffices. The
twenty-four evening briefs are one-liners, region-tagged, for events that
matter but do not bear expansion. This four-tier pyramid means the page can
be read at four depths: tab, numbers, heroes, everything.

**The date-check footer.** The footer exists because "one day" is the
contest's whole premise, and news is full of carryover: trials that began
earlier, negotiations that resumed, records set the night before. The footer
explicitly separates events that *happened* on 19 November 2026 from
developments *reported* on that day, so a reader - or a grader - can trust
that the page is not padding the day with recycled content. It also anchors
the bottom edge of the composition, balancing the masthead at the top and
closing the broadsheet form.
